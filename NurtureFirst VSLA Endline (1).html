How VSLA members are faring in Kisumu, Mombasa and Murang'a

Summary

Members in all three counties report better savings, easier credit and more confidence with money than before they joined. Almost everyone plans to stay. Kisumu is ahead on savings size and income. Mombasa's groups are the youngest and report steady but small gains. Murang'a lags on most outcomes, and its groups meet monthly rather than weekly.

Outcome scorecard

Share of respondents giving the positive answer on each indicator. Dots are counties; the dark tick is the overall figure. A p<.05 tag means the county difference is statistically significant (chi-square test).

County profiles

Income

Nearly 9 in 10 members say their income has gone up, but mostly "slightly". Murang'a has the most members reporting no change or a fall, and 38% there earn under KES 2,000 a month.

Savings

Saving is near-universal and runs almost entirely through the group. The size and rhythm of saving split sharply by county and follow each county's meeting schedule.

Loans

The VSLA is members' lender: 97% of recent loans came from the group. Access is easier everywhere. Repayment is the weak spot in Kisumu, where 19% of borrowers often struggle to repay.

Financial skills and confidence

Training coverage is high in Mombasa and Murang'a; about one in five Kisumu members has had none. Confidence rose almost everywhere.

Formal, digital and government services

Digital use is mostly M-Pesa. Bank accounts are rare outside Kisumu. Most members know of government programmes and would join one, but few have benefited, except in Murang'a.

What members need now

Larger loans top the list in every county. Mombasa members also stand out on business training and market access.
